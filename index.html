The paragraph

People often say they learn best in one particular way. The most popular model, called , sorts learners into four styles: learners, who prefer diagrams, charts and colour; learners, who remember what they hear in discussions and explanations; learners, who like notes, lists and textbooks; and learners, who understand things by doing, building and moving. Most of us have a favourite, and it is useful to know what it is, because it tells us how we like to take information in. However, scientists who have tested this idea found something surprising: teaching someone only in their “style” does not actually make them learn more. What really matters is how the brain builds memory. Learning happens in three steps: we new information by paying attention to it, we it by connecting it to things we already know, and we strengthen it every time we it from memory. That is why the methods that work for everyone are the same: over days instead of cramming, instead of re-reading, mixing pictures with words, and getting enough so the brain can file what it learned. In other words, your learning style is your preference, but good learning habits are what truly help you learn.

The four styles in VARK

Everyone leans towards one or two. None of them is better than the others.

Visual

Likes diagrams, colour, maps and charts. Remembers where things were on the page.

Try: turn your notes into a mind map.

Auditory

Remembers explanations, discussions and rhythm. Talks ideas through.

Try: explain the topic out loud to someone.

Reading & writing

Likes lists, definitions and textbooks. Learns by writing it down in their own words.

Try: rewrite the lesson as ten bullet points.

Kinaesthetic

Understands by doing: building, moving, experimenting, taking things apart.

Try: act it out or build a model.

What the brain actually does

Whatever your preference, every memory goes through the same three steps.

  1. 1

    Encode

    Pay attention. If you weren't really listening, nothing goes in.

  2. 2

    Store

    Connect the new idea to something you already know, so it has a place to live.

  3. 3

    Retrieve

    Pull it back out. Every time you do, the memory gets stronger.

Habits that work for everyone

  • Space it out

    Three short sessions on different days beat one long cram the night before.

  • Test yourself

    Close the book and try to recall it. Re-reading feels easier, but it fools you.

  • Mix pictures with words

    A sketch next to a sentence gives your brain two ways back to the idea.

  • Sleep

    Your brain files the day's learning while you sleep. Skip it and the files get lost.
